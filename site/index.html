Spotify drops every podcast you follow into one long pile. Podcast Manager splits it into playlists you design, like today’s news for the commute or the next chapter of your serial, and rebuilds them for you every day.

Set it up once, then just press play

You make the playlists and choose which shows go in them. The app does the rest in your own Spotify account, so you listen in the Spotify app on your phone, car or speaker, the way you already do.

  1. Make a playlist

    Give it a name like Morning or Long walks, and choose how it’s laid out: shows in the order you pick, or everything by release date.

  2. Add your shows

    Choose which podcasts go in, and how many episodes each one gives: the latest only, a few, or everything you haven’t heard.

  3. Listen in Spotify

    A matching playlist shows up in Spotify and is rebuilt each morning. Episodes you finish disappear from it through the day.

What a Tuesday morning looks like

The Morning playlist is set to take just the latest episode of each show. So three news shows give you today’s three episodes, and yesterday’s news never piles up.

Deep Water is a story told in order, so it’s marked as a serial. Instead of the newest episode, it gives you the next one you haven’t heard. Finish Episode 4 on the train, and Episode 5 is waiting tomorrow.

The same show can follow different rules in different playlists. Your Long walks playlist could take every unheard episode of it instead.

Small rules that save a lot of scrolling

Everything below happens on its own once your playlists are set up.

Serials stay in order

Mark a story podcast as a serial and it always gives you the next episode you haven’t heard, even in a playlist sorted newest first.

Finished episodes clear themselves

Every half hour, episodes you’ve played to the end are taken out of your playlists, so the top of the list is always something new.

Fresh every morning

Each day, at a time you choose, the app checks which shows you follow and rebuilds every playlist from what you haven’t heard yet.

One show, many playlists

A show can sit in several playlists with a different rule in each: just the latest in one, the whole back catalogue in another.

Unfollowing is handled gently

Stop following a show in Spotify and it stops appearing in your playlists straight away. It’s only removed for good after a week, so if you follow it again, it goes back where it was.

Tidy up without unfollowing

Archive a show you still follow but don’t want to see in the app. You can also turn a playlist off, and it won’t be touched until you turn it back on.

Use playlists you already have

Point the app at a Spotify playlist you already own instead of making a new one. It will only let you choose playlists that belong to you.

An iPhone app too

A companion iPhone app lets you check your playlists, add or remove shows, mark serials and start a rebuild. Creating and fine-tuning playlists happens in the web app. It isn’t on the App Store, so it needs building with Xcode.

What you need

Podcast Manager isn’t an app you download from a store. You run your own copy, and it looks after one Spotify account. Here’s what that involves.

  • A Spotify accountFree or Premium, the one you already listen with.
  • A computer that stays onA home server, a NAS, a Raspberry Pi-class machine or a small cloud server that can run Docker.
  • Your own Spotify developer appIt’s free and takes a few minutes on Spotify’s developer site. It’s how the app gets permission to manage your playlists.
  • A web address with HTTPSSpotify only lets you sign in through a secure address, so the app needs one.

Not sure what any of that means?

If you have a friend who runs a home server, this is an afternoon job for them. The setup guide on GitHub walks through each step.
